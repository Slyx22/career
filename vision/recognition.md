# Recognition: making a 70+ certificate mean something

**Goal:** a young person with a 70+ certificate feels proud and hopeful,
and employers genuinely treat it as a signal worth looking at.

The first half (feeling) we can design today. The second half (employers
recognising it) has to be earned with evidence. If we claim it before
it's true, we mislead young people who are already struggling, and we
break the Consumer Protection Act (false or misleading representations,
s41). So we use honest motivation now and build real recognition step by step.

## Live now

| Feature | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Score bands: Early stage, Developing, Strong match (70+), Excellent match (85+) | Turns a number into an identity people can aim for |
| "Strong match" seal on 70+ certificates, PDF, share images, LinkedIn entry | A visible badge of achievement; worth sharing |
| "14 points to a Strong match certificate" progress bar for scores under 70 | Goal-gradient effect: people work harder the closer a goal looks, and the next steps show how |
| Next steps ranked by points gained | Makes improvement feel doable today |
| Verifiable link + QR code on every certificate | Anyone can check it's genuine, which is what makes a credential worth anything |
| Certificates expire after 12 months | Keeps the seal meaningful; gives a reason to come back |

Wording rules (in Terms §2): a band describes how well the CV matched the
job's skills. It is our assessment, not an employer endorsement.

## Honest motivation: do and don't

Do:
- Celebrate progress ("You moved from 56 to 71").
- Show real numbers once we have them ("2,300 young people hold a Strong
  match certificate").
- Use the person's own goal ("for Accounts Clerk").

Don't:
- Say "employers trust" / "recognised by employers" until Phase 5 evidence exists.
- Show fake counters, fake scarcity or invented testimonials.
- Lower the bar for the seal to make more people feel good: if everyone
  has it, it means nothing.

## Path to real recognition

1. **Published method.** A public "How the score works" page linking the
   benchmark results and, later, the recruiter study.
2. **Partner pledges.** Organisations agree in writing to look at 70+
   certificate holders for entry-level roles. Only partners who signed
   appear as "Organisations that look at Strong match candidates".
3. **Employer signals.** In the employer platform, show candidates when
   their certificate was viewed or shortlisted (with their consent).
4. **Outcome stats.** Publish the interview and hire rate of 70+ holders
   vs others (evaluation.md, Level 4). Only then use "recognised".
5. **Institutional backing.** Approach Harambee / SA Youth, NYDA, SETAs
   and university career offices to reference the certificate in their
   programmes.

Only when steps 2 and 4 are true should the certificate say "recognised by …".
